Closed vs open models

Price, speed, quality: pick per task, not per assistant.

Running everything on a frontier API is easy and expensive. Running everything locally is free and often not good enough. Umnik is where I find the line, task by task: what does each job cost, how long does it take, and is the answer good enough?

Planning

Ambiguous requests and multi-step plans go to the frontier model. Quality matters most here. A flat-rate subscription keeps the bill predictable.

Everyday subtasks

Summaries, lookups, photo descriptions: a 9B open model on the Mac is fast enough and costs nothing per call.

Code and review

A 20B open model handles coding and review passes locally.

The real constraint is memory, not money. The Mac has 36 GB of unified memory, so at most two local helpers run at once. Hardware, not the API bill, sets the limit.

The journey

Rebuilt once, on purpose.

Umnik started on OpenClaw, an open-source agent framework. This year I moved it to Hermes Agent: the scheduled routines came along, and OpenClaw stays archived as a fallback.

The move also let me split the work between a closed planner and open local models, instead of sending everything to one API.
